\section{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

Assessing rice pollen viability is important for understanding the reproductive potential of rice plants and supporting research in plant breeding. However, conventional pollen viability assessment commonly involves staining pollen grains and manually examining and counting them under a microscope, a process that can be labor-intensive and time-consuming, particularly when large numbers of grains must be evaluated~\cite{tan2022,ascari2020}. To address the need for automated assessment, Go et al.~\cite{go2019} developed an image-analysis protocol for identifying viable and nonviable pollen in selected rice genotypes. Their method employed edge detection, dilation, region filling, and $k$-means clustering to identify individual pollen grains and classify their viability based on grayscale intensity. Although the method demonstrated promising results, its performance was affected by the presence of overlapping or clustered pollen grains. As reported in the study, accuracy decreased from 98.3\% for images without lumped pollen to 85.06\% for images containing lumped pollen~\cite{go2019}. This limitation suggests that conventional image-processing techniques may struggle to distinguish individual grains when their boundaries are difficult to identify.

Subsequent studies have explored machine learning and deep learning to improve automated pollen detection, segmentation, and viability classification. For instance, researchers have applied instance segmentation to pollen germination analysis and developed deep-learning models for pollen viability recognition~\cite{zhang2023,tan2022}. These approaches demonstrate the potential of learning-based methods to automate pollen analysis and accommodate challenging image conditions. Nevertheless, based on the literature reviewed in this study, the effectiveness of these methods for classifying rice pollen viability under overlapping or clustered conditions remains insufficiently established. In particular, it remains necessary to determine whether a model can distinguish individual rice pollen grains and classify their viability reliably when their boundaries overlap, and whether its performance under these conditions improves upon that of the existing image-analysis protocol. Addressing this gap requires an approach that can learn relevant visual features from annotated images rather than relying solely on manually selected image-processing parameters.

To address this problem, this study proposes the fine-tuning of a pretrained YOLOv8n-seg instance-segmentation model to detect individual rice pollen grains, generate a separate segmentation mask for each detected grain, and classify each grain as viable or nonviable. The proposed model will be evaluated against the image-analysis protocol of Go et al.~\cite{go2019} using stained microscopic images from six rice genotypes. Both methods will be assessed separately on images with and without lumped pollen to determine how overlapping grains affect their performance. Detection and classification metrics, together with comparisons between automated viability estimates and manual counts, will be used to evaluate the proposed approach. Through this comparison, the study seeks to determine whether instance segmentation offers a more reliable approach to automated rice pollen viability classification, particularly in images containing overlapping or clustered grains.
