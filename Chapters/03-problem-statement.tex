\section{Problem Statement}
\label{ch:problem-statement}

Rice pollen viability is commonly assessed by manually counting stained grains under a microscope, which is a slow and labor-intensive process. Go et al.~\cite{go2019} automated this task using an edge-based image-analysis protocol, but accuracy decreased from 98.3\% to 85.06\% when pollen grains overlapped or clustered. Although deep-learning methods have improved the separation of overlapping pollen in other species~\cite{zhang2023, tan2022, li2026pvdt}, they have not been evaluated on rice pollen viability classification. Therefore, there remains a need for an automated method that can reliably classify rice pollen viability even when grains overlap.

Specifically, this study seeks to answer the following questions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item How accurately can a YOLO-based instance-segmentation model detect individual rice pollen grains and classify them as viable or nonviable?
    \item How does the model's performance on images without lumped pollen compare with its performance on images with lumped pollen?
    \item How does the model compare with the edge-based protocol of Go et al.~\cite{go2019}, particularly on images containing lumped pollen?
    \item How closely do the model's estimated viability percentages agree with manual pollen counts?
\end{enumerate}
